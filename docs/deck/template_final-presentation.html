The premise
02 — Motivation

Why give up
position control?

A position policy is stiff — the paper calls it “nearly unable to be displaced without stepping.” It fights every disturbance. A torque policy yields: it absorbs an external force and recovers, the way a real muscle does.

Torque control buys compliance — but raw torque is dangerous to hardware. SATA's bet: borrow the body's own safety limits from biology.

The system
03 — Architecture

A learned torque policy, wrapped in a biomechanical layer

The policy outputs raw joint torque. Three bio-inspired stages reshape it into something a real motor could survive — each independently ablatable.

stage 01

Activation

First-order low-pass on torque sign — models muscle-recruitment delay, kills bang-bang switching.

stage 02

Hill model

Force–velocity law: max torque drops when the joint moves fast in the same direction.

stage 03

Fatigue

Leaky accumulator per joint, fed back into the observation. Small −0.05 reward penalty.

curriculum

Growth

Gompertz ramp: front-leg torque ceiling 0.3→1.0, control rate 100→200 Hz.

Phase 2 · Ablation
06 — A counter-intuitive result

Removing the bio
constraints raises
reward.

On training-distribution reward, dropping fatigue or activation makes the policy score higher. Taken alone, that looks like the bio layer is dead weight.

This is the expected sign of a working sim-to-real constraint: a bound that protects hardware costs a little reward in a clean simulator. Whether the cost is worth paying can't be settled here — only out-of-distribution, in Phase 3.

Trap avoided: "higher reward ⇒ better mechanism" is the wrong inference. Reward in a frictionless sim doesn't price hardware risk.

Synthesis · The original framing
10 — Two philosophies of adaptation

We let adaptive control supply the questions.

Not "SATA is adaptive control" — it has no Lyapunov law, no online parameter estimator. The lens is for contrast, and the data corrects two tempting analogies.

Takeaway
11 — What the four phases settle

Bio mechanisms are a
feasibility envelope,
not a reward device.

  • Reproduced SATA honestly — including the harder 8-seed variance the first 3 seeds hid.
  • Ablations that "win" reward do so only by leaving the hardware-realisable torque envelope.
  • A classical residual recovers some lost payload capability within the envelope — but only modestly.
  • The ceiling on the bolt-on motivates co-designed RL + adaptation.
  • Limits kept in view: simulation only · no thermal model · residual gains tuned on one seed · the residual's payload gain is a non-significant trend (p≈0.07).
  • Open question: with all bio mechanisms off, our policy still trained fine — but our ablation degenerates to scaled raw torque (not necessarily the paper's baseline, which it does not release), so we leave the trainability question unresolved, not answered.
