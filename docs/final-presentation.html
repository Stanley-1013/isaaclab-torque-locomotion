The system
02 — What SATA is

A learned torque policy, wrapped in a biomechanical layer

SATA outputs raw joint torque, not position targets. A bio-inspired layer reshapes that torque into something a real motor could survive — keeping the policy hardware-feasible for zero-shot sim-to-real.

stage 01

Activation

First-order low-pass on the torque command — models muscle-recruitment delay, smooths bang-bang switching.

stage 02

Hill model

Force–velocity law: the available torque drops when a joint moves fast in the same direction.

stage 03

Fatigue

Leaky per-joint accumulator, fed back into the observation, with a small reward penalty.

curriculum

Growth

Gompertz ramp over training: torque ceiling 7→23.5 N·m, control rate 100→200 Hz.

SATA: Li et al., RSS 2025 · arXiv:2502.12674 · the growth curriculum is the method's namesake.
The question
03 — A prior, narrower question

Does a faithful port
survive an engine change?

We already reproduced SATA in Isaac Gym (a sibling repo) — that is our ground truth. Here we ask something narrower: hold the robot fixed (Go2), change only the simulator, and see whether the policy still reproduces.

Isaac Gym (PhysX 4, legged_gym) → Isaac Lab (PhysX 5, manager-based RL). Keeping the robot fixed means any mismatch is attributable to the engine / port, not the robot.
A key sub-migration: Isaac Lab's stock Go2 ships as a position-PD controller. SATA is torque / effort control — so the actuator model itself had to be ported, not just the config.
cross-engine robot fixed position-PD → effort reproducibility
Ground truth = the Isaac Gym reproduction (go2_torque), converging to mean reward ~104 at 3000 it.
Porting reality
04 — A direct port isn’t direct

A direct port
isn’t direct.

The porting bugs were mostly index / order / unit / accounting assumptions, not algorithm errors. Getting a faithful port took six fixes.

  • terrain was rough, not flat;
  • reward fed to PPO = a single substep;
  • variable-frequency control carries its residual over;
  • counters advance per substep;
  • calf velocity limit (15.70 → 20.07 rad/s);
  • SATA’s joint position limits written to the physics sim.
An early bug — a 2×-over-scaled forward reward made the robot belly-crawl instead of walk. Shown honestly: this is what a wrong port looks like before the fixes.
Full provenance for each fix in docs/REPRODUCTION_NOTES.md (pitfalls + what closed the gap).
The fix that mattered most
05 — Limits written to the sim

SATA’s joint position limits were only used in the reward and termination, never written to the physics sim. The wider stock-USD thigh limit let exploration over-extend the thigh and trip the hard-limit termination on most episodes. Writing SATA’s limits to the sim produced a clean bent-leg gait.

Per-step decomposition
07 — Where the gap lives

The task terms
match.

Removing the episode-length confound (per-step = return / episode length), the 0.76 reward ratio factors into ~10% shorter episodes × ~16% lower per-step reward.

Splitting per-step by reward term: the task / positive terms (forward, head-height, lateral, yaw tracking) match the Gym reference to within ~0.002/step. On the axis we measured, the policy reproduces SATA’s per-step task performance.
The per-step deficit is ~93% one penalty term, joint_acc (Lab −0.0194 vs Gym −0.0111 / step) — which has a documented cross-engine definitional difference, below.

Honest limits
08 — What we do not claim

We do not claim the
engines are equivalent.

The open questions we leave honestly, rather than paper over.

  • We have not separated “the penalty measures acceleration differently” from “the Lab gait is genuinely jerkier.” Confirming that would need joint-acceleration logged under one matched definition on both engines.
  • The ~10% episode-length shortfall (Lab 1767 vs Gym 1959 steps) is left as a real, unexplained residual — not attributed to a cause we have not isolated.
  • Rendering caveat: the native Isaac Sim RTX renderer crashes on our container (a known NVIDIA driver-595 ↔ rtx.scenedb bug).
  • Every clip here is therefore a kinematic replay of the Isaac-Lab-trained trajectory, rendered in Isaac Gym — the joint states are real, only the rendering engine differs (labelled as such).
The matched positive terms make “the policy did not learn the task” implausible — but we stop short of calling the joint_acc residual purely a measurement difference, because we have not yet logged both engines under one definition.
Caveats kept in view: sim-only · cross-engine · one penalty term unresolved.
Takeaway
09 — What this reproduction settles

A direct port walks
in a different engine.

  • On the axis we measured, the port reproduces SATA’s per-step task performance under matched config.
  • The residual reward-number gap traces mostly to one penalty term with a known cross-engine definitional difference.
  • Reproducibility carried across engines — including the failure mode: a collapsed seed appears on both.
  • Limits: sim-only · cross-engine · the joint_acc definitional question is left unresolved.
